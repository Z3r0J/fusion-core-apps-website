To file a warranty claim, confirm the product is still covered, gather your proof of purchase and serial number, and submit a claim to whoever issued the warranty (the store, the manufacturer, or the extended-plan provider) with a factual description and photos of the defect. After that, the warranty claim process is mostly paperwork: save the claim number, follow up until the product is repaired, replaced, or refunded, and escalate in writing if the claim is denied.

## The Warranty Claim Process at a Glance

| Step | What to do | What you need |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Check coverage | Confirm the purchase date plus the warranty length still puts you inside the window, and that your fault is not excluded | The receipt or order email, the warranty terms |
| 2. Gather documents | Collect everything the claim will ask for before you start | Proof of purchase, model and serial number, photos or video of the defect, a short timeline |
| 3. Pick the right party | Store warranty → the retailer. Manufacturer warranty → the brand. Extended plan → the provider named on the contract | The warranty card or contract, which names the "warrantor" |
| 4. Find the official channel | Use the claim form, service request, mail-in repair, or support email listed on the warrantor's own website | The brand's or store's support page |
| 5. Submit the claim | Fill in the claim form, describe the defect factually, attach your files, and say which remedy you want | Everything from step 2 |
| 6. Save and follow up | Record the claim number, the date, and who you spoke to; chase it when the promised response time passes | The claim or case number, the email thread |
| 7. Escalate if denied | Get the reason in writing, re-read the terms, go up the chain, then use dispute programs, regulators, or small claims court | The written denial and your full paper trail |

## What You Need Before You File

A claim moves faster when you have everything in one place first. Gather:

- **Proof of purchase.** The receipt or invoice showing the product, price, store, and purchase date. This is the single most important document — the purchase date is what starts the warranty clock.
- **The product serial or model number.** Usually on the product itself, its box, or a sticker on the back or underside.
- **The warranty terms.** The card in the box or the terms on the manufacturer's website, so you know exactly what is covered and for how long.
- **Evidence of the defect.** Photos or a short video of the problem, and a note of when it started.

Often the reason a claim stalls has nothing to do with the fault itself — it is that the customer cannot produce a legible receipt. Thermal receipts fade, and paperwork gets lost, so the person who kept a copy is the person who gets the repair. A card statement, an order email, or a store's purchase lookup can often stand in; our proof-of-purchase guide lists [7 places to look when the receipt is gone](/blog/proof-of-purchase-warranty-lost-receipt).

## Step by Step: How to File a Warranty Claim

1. **Check the coverage window.** Find the purchase date and the warranty length, and confirm you are still inside it. File the moment you notice a problem — waiting gives the company room to argue about when the fault started.
2. **Decide who to contact.** A store warranty goes to the retailer; a manufacturer warranty goes to the brand. If you are unsure, start with whichever coverage is longer or better matches the fault.
3. **Find the claim channel.** Most manufacturers have a support page or a "warranty claim" form on their site. If you cannot find it, searching the brand name plus "warranty claim" usually lands on the right page — just make sure it is the brand's own domain.
4. **Describe the defect factually.** State when it started, what happens, and what you have already tried. Mention that the product was used normally. Attach your photos and proof of purchase.
5. **Say what you want.** Ask for a repair, a replacement, or a refund. Expect a repair first: the FTC notes that a company may have the right to fix a product before it refunds your money.
6. **Submit and save the confirmation.** Keep the claim number, the date, and any email thread. If you called, note the agent's name and the reference number. Do not ship the product anywhere until you have a claim or return authorization number.
7. **Follow up if you do not hear back.** Coverage terms often promise a response or resolution within a set number of days; if that passes, chase it with your claim number in hand. Reporting inside the window is what counts: per the FTC, if you report a defect during the warranty period and it is not properly fixed, the company must correct it even if the warranty expires before the repair is done.

## What to Include in a Warranty Claim Form

Online warranty claim forms differ in layout, but they ask for almost the same fields. Have these ready before you open the form, because many time out:

| Field | What to enter | Tip |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Your details | Name, email, phone, and an address for in-home service or a shipping label | Use the email you bought with, if you can |
| Product | Product name, model number, serial number | Photograph the serial label; it is usually on the back, bottom, or inside the door |
| Purchase | Purchase date, seller, order or receipt number | The date is what gets checked against the warranty length |
| Proof of purchase | An upload of the receipt, invoice, or order confirmation | A clear photo or PDF; a scanned copy works if the paper has faded |
| Problem type | The closest category the form offers (won't power on, leaking, broken part…) | Pick the nearest match and explain the rest in the description |
| Description | When it started, what happens, error codes, what you tried | Keep it factual and say the product was used normally |
| Evidence | Photos or a short video of the defect | Show the fault and the serial label |
| Resolution | Repair, replacement, or refund | The company may try a repair first |
| Registration | Whether the product was registered | Only a "limited" warranty can make registration a condition of service |

## Store Warranty vs. Manufacturer Warranty: Who to Contact

A single product can be covered by more than one warranty at the same time, each with its own clock. A **store warranty** is offered by the retailer that sold you the item. A **manufacturer warranty** comes from the brand that made it, and often runs longer. Some purchases also carry an **extended warranty** you paid extra for; claims on that go to the provider named on the contract.

Read all of them before you file. If the manufacturer warranty covers your specific fault for longer, contacting the brand directly may save a step. If the retailer handled the sale and offers on-the-spot exchanges, the store may be faster. There is no universal rule — the right party is whoever covers *your* fault for the longest, with the least friction.

## What to Do If Your Warranty Claim Is Denied

A denial is not always the end of the road. The steps below follow US federal law; outside the US, start with your national consumer-protection agency.

### Find out exactly why

- **Get the reason in writing.** A vague "not covered" is not enough — ask which clause the decision is based on.
- **Check it against the usual reasons.** Typical ones are the claim arriving after coverage ended, no proof of purchase, damage from an accident or misuse, a part the warranty excludes (wear items, cosmetic damage), or a modification.
- **Re-read the terms.** Denials are sometimes simply wrong, or based on an exclusion that does not actually apply to your situation.

### Know your rights under US law

Written warranties on consumer products are governed by the **Magnuson-Moss Warranty Act**, enforced by the Federal Trade Commission (FTC). The points that matter most in a dispute:

- **A defect reported in time must be fixed.** If you reported it during the warranty period and it was not properly repaired, the company must still correct it, even after the warranty expires.
- **Third-party parts or repairs do not void coverage by themselves.** A manufacturer cannot require you to use specific parts or service providers to keep the warranty unless it provides them free or has an FTC waiver. In April 2018, FTC staff warned six companies that sell cars, cellphones, and video game systems about exactly this. A company can still refuse to cover damage that a third-party part actually caused; for cars, the FTC says the manufacturer has to prove it.
- **Registration is not always required.** A "full" warranty cannot require you to return a registration card; a "limited" warranty can.
- **Implied warranties still apply.** State laws create implied warranties that cover almost everything you buy unless it was sold "as is" where that is allowed, and they can last up to four years in some states.
- **You can go to court.** Breach of a written warranty is a violation of federal law, and a consumer who wins can recover court costs and reasonable attorneys' fees.

### Escalate in this order

1. **Go up the chain.** Ask for a supervisor, or go over the retailer's head to the manufacturer if the store is the one refusing. The FTC suggests writing to the manufacturer and sending the letter by certified mail with a return receipt.
2. **Use the warranty's dispute program, if it has one.** Some warranties require you to try it before you sue under Magnuson-Moss. A program that follows the FTC's dispute rule must decide within 40 days, and its decision is not binding on you.
3. **Report the company.** File a report with the FTC at ReportFraud.ftc.gov and with your state attorney general.
4. **Take it to small claims court.** It fits the cost of a repair or replacement. For a large claim, talk to a consumer-protection attorney, and check your purchase terms for an arbitration clause first. The FTC's position is that a written warranty can't require binding arbitration: any dispute program a warranty requires must leave you free to go to court, as its [Businessperson's Guide to Federal Warranty Law](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/businesspersons-guide-federal-warranty-law) explains.

This is exactly why the documentation you gathered before filing matters: a well-organized claim with a clear timeline is far harder to dismiss than a vague complaint.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I file a warranty claim?

Confirm the product is still within its warranty period, gather your proof of purchase, model and serial number, and photos of the defect, then submit a claim to whoever issued the warranty: the retailer for a store warranty, the brand for a manufacturer warranty, or the provider named on an extended plan. Save the claim number and follow up until it is resolved.

### What is a warranty claim?

A warranty claim is a request to the company that issued a warranty to repair, replace, or refund a product that developed a covered defect during the warranty period. The company checks your proof of purchase and the defect against the warranty terms before it approves the claim.

### Do I need a receipt to claim a warranty?

In most cases yes. The receipt is the proof of purchase that shows what you bought and when the warranty started. If you have lost the paper receipt, a bank or card statement, an order confirmation email, or a scanned copy stored in a warranty tracker can often stand in.

### How long does a warranty claim take?

It depends on the company and the fix. A replacement part can ship within days, while a mail-in or in-home repair can take weeks. Ask for an estimate when you file and note it next to your claim number. If the warranty sends disputes to a program that follows the FTC's dispute rule, that program has to decide within 40 days.

### Do I have to register a product to get warranty service?

Not always. In the US, the FTC says a full warranty cannot require you to return a registration card, but a limited warranty can make registration a condition of service. Check the warranty terms. Registering is still useful because it puts your purchase on record with the brand.

### Does using an independent repair shop void my warranty?

Keep your full paper trail.

## Takeaways

Filing a warranty claim comes down to three things: proof you bought it, proof of the defect, and doing it before the clock runs out. Gather your documents first, contact the party whose coverage fits your fault, and keep a record of everything you send. Do that, and the only claims you lose are the ones that were never covered in the first place.
