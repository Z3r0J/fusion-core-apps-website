To file a manufacturer warranty claim, go to the brand's support or warranty page, have your **proof of purchase**, **model and serial number**, and **photos of the defect** ready, then submit the online claim form (or email or call support). Save the claim number and follow up if the promised response time passes.

That covers most claims. Below are the details that make the difference between a fast approval and a back-and-forth — plus an email template you can copy.

## Store or Manufacturer: Who Should You Contact?

| Situation | Contact |
| --- | --- |
| Still inside the store's return/exchange window | The **store** — usually the fastest fix |
| Past the return window, inside the manufacturer warranty | The **manufacturer** (the brand) |
| You bought an extended warranty or protection plan | The **plan provider** named on the contract |
| Both warranties cover the fault | Whichever has less friction (often the brand for appliances, the store for small electronics) |
| You live in the EU or UK | The **store** remains legally responsible for faults present at delivery (at least 2 years in the EU), even after its return window |

When in doubt, check the warranty card in the box or the brand's website: it states who the "warrantor" is and how to reach them.

## Step 1: Gather Everything Before You Start

Claim forms time out, and support agents ask the same questions every time. Have these ready:

- **Proof of purchase** — receipt, invoice, order email, or card statement. Lost it? See [how to get proof of purchase without a receipt](/blog/proof-of-purchase-warranty-lost-receipt).
- **Model number and serial number** — usually on a label on the back, bottom, or inside the door (appliances), or in the device's settings (electronics).
- **Purchase date and seller.**
- **Photos or a short video of the fault**, plus any error codes on the display.
- **A one-paragraph description**: when it started, what happens, what you already tried.

## Step 2: Find the Official Claim Channel

Search for the brand name plus "warranty claim" or "service request" and make sure you land on the brand's **own domain**. Most manufacturers offer one of these:

- An **online warranty claim form** (the fastest and best documented).
- A **service request** for appliances, which schedules a technician.
- A **mail-in repair** process for small devices, with a shipping label.
- A **support email or phone line**.

Avoid third-party "warranty claim" sites that ask for payment upfront. A genuine manufacturer warranty should not charge a fee just to open a claim, but check the terms: some limited warranties do charge for shipping or labor.

## Step 3: Describe the Defect So It Reads as Covered

Warranties cover **defects in materials or workmanship** under normal use. Write your description factually and in those terms:

- ✅ "The compressor stopped cooling on 2 October. The fridge is at 15°C with the display showing error E5. It has been used normally in a kitchen since purchase."
- ❌ "This fridge is junk and broke for no reason."

State that the product was used normally and not modified. If you already tried basic troubleshooting from the manual, say so — it saves a round of questions.

## Step 4: Submit, Then Keep a Paper Trail

After you submit, record the **claim or case number**, the **date**, and the **name of anyone you spoke to**. Keep every email in one thread. If the brand promises a response within a set number of days and that passes, follow up with your claim number in the first line.

## Manufacturer Warranty Claim Email Template

Copy this, replace the brackets, and attach your proof of purchase and photos.

```text
Subject: Warranty claim – [Product name, model number] – Serial [serial number]

Hello [Brand] Support,

I am submitting a warranty claim for my [product name], model [model number],
serial number [serial number], purchased from [store] on [purchase date].

Defect: Since [date], the [product] [describe what happens, including any error
code]. It has been used normally for its intended purpose and has not been
modified or repaired by a third party. I have already tried [troubleshooting
steps, if any].

The product is within its [length]-year manufacturer warranty. Attached are:
- Proof of purchase ([receipt / invoice / order confirmation])
- Photos/video of the defect
- Photo of the model and serial number label

Please let me know the next steps for repair or replacement and the expected
timeframe. I can be reached at [phone] or by replying to this email.

Thank you,
[Your full name]
[Address, if an in-home repair or shipping label is needed]
```

## If the Claim Is Denied

Ask for the reason **in writing** and which warranty clause it relies on. Re-read the terms — denials are sometimes based on exclusions that do not apply. Escalate to a supervisor, and if you still believe the decision is wrong, your local consumer-protection agency can review it. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### How do I file a manufacturer warranty claim?

Find the brand's warranty or support page, have your proof of purchase, model and serial number, and photos of the defect ready, then submit the online warranty claim form or contact support by email or phone. Keep the claim or case number and follow up if you do not get a response in the promised time.

### Should I contact the store or the manufacturer?

Within the store's return or exchange window, the store is usually faster. After that, the manufacturer warranty is normally the one to use, so contact the brand directly. In the EU and UK, the seller also stays legally responsible for faults present at delivery, so the store remains an option there. If both cover the fault, choose the one with less friction.

### What information does a warranty claim form ask for?

Typically your contact details, the product model and serial number, the purchase date and seller, a proof-of-purchase file, a description of the fault, and sometimes photos or a video. Some brands also ask whether the product was registered.

### How long does a manufacturer warranty claim take?

It depends on the brand and the fix. A simple replacement part can ship within days; an in-home appliance repair or a mail-in device repair can take one to several weeks. Ask for an estimate when you file and note it with your claim number.
